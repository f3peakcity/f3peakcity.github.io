> It is said that an idea can be a dangerous thing. 

Your humble correspondent has had the idea of doing the [Pilot Mountain to Hanging Rock Ultra](https://www.pm2hrultras.com) for a few years now, but because the race happens the weekend after the Blue Ridge Relay each year (and because YHC is not quite as tough as Steaks, though often as shirtless), this same correspondent had yet to tackle the race. 

![Dan River Sign](/img/backblasts/pmhr/sign.jpg)

Then back in the spring, perhaps suffering under the pain of the fourth iteration of the Hill Climb Challenge, none of the pax seemed enthusiastic about doing BRR again this year. And so YHC thought it might be the perfect year to run from one iconic NC landmark to the other. But for some reason, no one else wanted to commit to a 50-mile ultra. So the dangerous idea became a little less dangerous by morphing into a 4-man relay team with Clockwork, Imp, I-Beam, and Rooney. And then it became a little more fun when Jockey, Gibraltar, Ramsay, and Mr. Safety formed a second team.

## The race experience

Every good odyssey starts _in medias res_, so I will first tease you with a picture of the mountains calling. 

![Hanging Rock mountain top](/img/backblasts/pmhr/hr-top.jpg)

Hopefully you agree that this picture was worth it. It may have cost us the race. But more on that later.

### Getting there

Inundated by so many emails and pre-race organizer meetings that not even ChatGPT could summarize, it felt like the day would never come.

But on Friday, despite Town of Apex construction lane closures, all the pax made it to Mr. Safety's slice of paradise to depart in an early and late van.

One of the best parts of any trip is the mumblechatter along the way, made even more dramatic when some of YHC's more morbid stories and 9/11 recollections were accented by the special effects of a lightning show and rainstorm.

But the storms cleared up within the 2-hr drive to the packet pickup, a beautiful place called the Green Heron Ale House along the Dan River.

### To Hanging Rock

The sun and heat were manageable throughout the middle of the race, but as we drew near Hanging Rock, the weather curiously began to repeat itself, with dark clouds drawing up.

![HR afar](/img/backblasts/pmhr/hr-far.jpg)

_Note that Imp assured YHC Hanging Rock was "not too far" at this point._

This was also the part of the race where we began to pass a lot of the 50k and 50mi participants, uttering a pathetic "Good job," as we breezed by them struggling being ~40mi in while we had been switching on and off and were relatively fresh. Someone's gotta be the bad guy, I guess.

After a grueling uphill climb, YHC would like to report that Hanging Rock got easier on the way down, but it actually got worse, with stone step drops that seemed endless. This is where YHC lost the second-place, and possibly first-place relay finish...if only I had swapped bibs with Mr. Safety, who crushed it.

![HR stairs](/img/backblasts/pmhr/hr-stairs.jpg)

End the stairs eventually did, as YHC heard the echoes of the aid station waft across the hazy lake. A lake that proved to be roped off at the last exchange. Probably for the best to spare I-Beam's super nice Expedition.

![HR beach](/img/backblasts/pmhr/hr-beach.jpg)

The last leg also proved to be a blistering one, but Rooney and Jockey pulled it off, with the whole rest of the evening left to enjoy.

## Takeaways

The relay course is the same as the PMHR 50-mile course, except you can split it up in any combination of 2- to 4-man, everyone just has to run 1 leg, order doesn't matter.

### Logistics

Logistics are much simpler vs. longer relays like the BRR.

| PMHR | Other relays like BRR |
| --- | --- |
| 50 miles total | 200 miles total |
| 2 legs/~12mi pp in 4-man | 3 legs/~17mi pp in 12-man |
| Shorter breaks between legs | Longer breaks between legs |
| Done ~10hrs | Done ~28-32hrs |
| Only a couple headlamp legs | Overnight legs |
| ~2hr drive | ~5hr drive |
| 1 SUV fits whole team | Often rent 2 passenger vans |
| ~$300 total cost pp | ~$600 total cost pp |

One thing that was _not_ as simple as the BRR was estimating the pace. Because it's a trail with a few legs that feature significant climbs, the pace predictions were harder to guess than simply taking a 10k time, or even our average Umstead trail run pace (~12min/mi).

### New stuff

Gibraltar introduced us to a **Splitwise** app to make sharing expenses a bit easier, especially since we had some team-specific expenses and some shared across everyone.

### Training

Summer is a special time in Peak City, when we bring in a bunch of seasonal AOs, many of which are calibrated to help you train for fall races.

YHC's personal training looked like:

- Tuesdays: Ovaltime speedwork
- Wednesdays: EC run then yoga at 7th Inning
- Thursdays: Half Dome hills (year round)
- Fridays: Paddleboarding...because everyone needs some rest!
- Saturdays: Umstead trails collab with the full team

And then I would try to get an afternoon run in either Thursdays or Sundays. YHC also intended to do strength training Mondays, but several minor hand surgeries and unexpected travel this summer set me back.

Overall, I think the team did well doing training runs together. Shoutout to Gibraltar for coming up with schedules, calendars, and taper plans.

If I could do it over, I would have us do team ruck stairclimbs...at least definitely for whoever runs Leg 3 (Pilot Mountain) and Leg 7 (Hanging Rock).

### In sum

In F3 we often say, "If you can do this, you can do the BRR." Even more so PMHR. It would be a very nice race to do if you haven't done the BRR or aren't sure you can commit to the full BRR experience. And it might be a nice way to do an ultra at the same times as other F3 guys do the relay.

I will also say on a personal note, this might have been the most upended summer I've had in F3 to date, what with a lot of personal stuff and travel going on. I really appreciate the team stepping in, taking over the last-minute logistics, and making it a great sort of welcome-back trip. Hopefully you won't come down too hard on me for not pushing it on my last leg...at least I wrote up the backblast this time (sorry Blackburns).
